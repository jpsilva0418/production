This question usually arrives framed as a budget decision, as though the two platforms were competing versions of the same product. They are not. They do different jobs, and for most local businesses the choice is decided by one question before anything else is considered.

## The question that decides it

**Do people already know they need what you sell?**

If someone's boiler fails at eleven at night, they do not need to be persuaded that boiler repair exists. They open their phone and search. The business that appears is the business that gets called. The demand already exists; the only question is who captures it.

That is Google. Search advertising intercepts an intention that is already formed.

Now take a med spa launching a new treatment. Nobody is searching for it, because they do not know it exists. There is no demand to capture — it has to be created, by showing the result to someone who was not looking for it.

That is Meta. Facebook and Instagram interrupt rather than answer.

Most local businesses sit clearly on one side of that line, and that decides the starting channel.

## Where Google is usually the right first move

**Urgency and emergencies.** Plumbing, roofing after a storm, locksmiths, auto repair, emergency dental. The search is the moment of need.

**Established, well-understood services.** Cleaning, landscaping, accounting, legal. People know these exist and search by name.

**High-consideration local services.** Someone researching a contractor for a kitchen remodel searches, compares and reads. You want to be in the comparison.

The advantage is intent. The disadvantage is cost: you are bidding against everyone else who wants that same customer, and in competitive trades a single click is not cheap. That is exactly why the landing page matters so much — expensive clicks make a weak page expensive too.

## Where Meta is usually the right first move

**Visual results.** Beauty, aesthetics, fitness, interiors, photography, food. A before-and-after stops a thumb in a way a text ad cannot.

**New or unfamiliar offers.** If you have to explain it before someone wants it, search cannot help — nobody is searching for the thing they have not heard of.

**Impulse and discretionary purchases.** Treatments, classes, events, seasonal offers.

**Retargeting, for everybody.** This is the exception that applies regardless of industry. Showing a relevant ad to someone who already visited your site is among the most sensible advertising a small business can run, and Meta does it cheaply.

The advantage is cost per impression and the strength of the targeting. The disadvantage is that you are interrupting, so the creative carries almost all of the weight. A mediocre ad on Meta does not underperform — it does nothing at all.

## What is true on both

**Conversion tracking is not optional.** Without it you are optimising for clicks, and both platforms will cheerfully deliver clicks that never become customers. Google Ads needs call and form conversions configured. Meta needs the Pixel and the Conversions API, verified end to end. Do this before spending, not after.

**The landing page decides the result.** Both channels buy attention. Neither improves what happens after the click. I have [written separately about why that order matters](/blog/fix-the-landing-page-before-running-ads).

**Accounts should be in your name.** Your Google Ads account, your Meta Business Manager, your pixel, your audiences. If an agency owns them, your campaign history and your retargeting audiences leave when the relationship does. This is non-negotiable and it costs nothing to get right at the start.

**Start small and read it honestly.** A small budget that runs long enough to produce real data tells you more than a large budget spent in a fortnight.

## If you are going to run only one

Start with the channel that matches your demand type, prove it works with real conversion data, and only then add the second.

For most trades and home services, that means Google first and Meta later for retargeting. For most beauty, wellness and visual-result businesses, it means Meta first and Google later for the people who search by name once they have heard of you.

The wrong answer is running both at half budget before either is tracked properly. That produces two campaigns with not enough data to judge, which is how people conclude that advertising does not work.
